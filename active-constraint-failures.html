Active-Constraint Failures in Learned AC Optimal Power Flow.

Average prediction accuracy can look strong while recovered power-flow solutions violate the limits that matter most. This study follows individual constraints into their binding regimes to explain when and why violations grow.

Damilola Agboola & Kasem Khalil
Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering · University of Mississippi · Oxford, Mississippi
AC-OPFActive constraintsLearned surrogatesFeasibility evaluation
The research question

What happens after a constraint binds?

Learned AC-OPF surrogates are often assessed through average setpoint errors. We instead examine the same constraint along a load path, starting at the point where the optimal solution reaches its limit. After reconstructing the physical AC state using power-flow recovery, we ask whether the surrogate's violation worsens as the operating point moves deeper into the binding regime.

Method

Measure the failure. Explain the mechanism.

01 / EVENT STUDY

Follow each binding constraint

Track a constraint through its activation on a load path and measure normalized violation depth after AC power-flow recovery. Compare within the same constraint when its optimal slack remains zero.

02 / UNDER-BENDING

Test whether models learn the kink

Compare the slopes of optimal and surrogate slack before and after activation. A model may track the approach to a bound while failing to turn with the optimal solution at the boundary.

03 / AC SENSITIVITY

Identify the cause of growth

Decompose linearized constraint violation into sensitivity magnitude, control-error magnitude, and their directional alignment to distinguish amplification from systematic error direction.

04 / PRACTICAL CORRECTION

Evaluate selective re-solving

Test whether selecting instances for optimization re-solving by predicted binding strength outperforms simpler choices based on load, random selection, or disagreement.

Principal findings

Better averages do not guarantee binding-regime feasibility.

FINDING / 01

Accuracy lowers the level, not the growth

A more accurate full-state MLP reduced reactive violation on the 300-bus study yet retained a depth slope close to the conventional MLP.

FINDING / 02

Under-bending explains the pattern

Surrogates frequently follow an approaching limit but do not reproduce the optimal change in slope when the constraint binds.

FINDING / 03

Alignment, not amplification

Recovered violations grow mainly because the surrogate's control errors increasingly point in a direction to which the binding constraint is sensitive, not because sensitivity magnitude increases.

FINDING / 04

Selective re-solving remains difficult

Selection using predicted binding strength did not outperform load-based selection under the study's prespecified comparison criteria.
